# Disney & Pixar question bank

The category contains 300 original multiple-choice questions about 70 animated films. Classic Disney contributes five questions per film across 20 titles; modern Disney and Pixar each contribute four per film across 25 titles. These writing batches form one selectable category.

The scope is the original animated film stories, including clearly named sequels. Book versions, television spin-offs, live-action remakes and Marvel/Star Wars are outside this category. Full-film spoilers are possible and the topic label says so. Films such as Pocahontas are treated as fictional film stories, not historical accounts.

## Question style

Questions favor familiar characters, choices, relationships, powers, objects and memorable scene outcomes. Each film contributes distinct facts; franchise sequels avoid repeating the same identity or power. Moderate challenges use story context rather than production credits, release dates or incidental numerical details. Every entry has four choices, one exact keyed answer and a short explanation.

The 300 correct answers are evenly distributed among four positions. Assembly deterministically shuffles those positions and the other options rather than leaving a repeated four-question pattern. New IDs begin `53000000-`; the seed appends them without modifying any released or archived content.

## Sources and review

Every question stores a film-specific source title and HTTPS URL. References are predominantly the relevant Wikipedia animated-film plot pages, with D23 articles supporting Stitch's Ugly Duckling inspiration and Destiny speaking whale in Finding Dory. Original wording paraphrases facts; no dialogue or lyric passages are reproduced.

Separate content reviewers read all 300 proposed questions and checked canon, unique keys, plausible choices, self-answering prompts and repeated or reversed facts. Review corrections included Dumbo's vision, Stinky Pete's secret sabotage, Russell's first visit to Carl, the immediate effect of Miguel's guitar theft, Cruz's dream of being a racer and a self-answering Brother Bear question. Distractors were refined for plausibility.

Film references and selected uncertain facts were checked with targeted searches and page reads. This was not a frame-by-frame verification of every answer or an independent audit of every source. Attribution and structural checks alone do not prove factual correctness. Sources are stable reference material, and ordinary CI makes no external reference-page requests.

The database checks validate the bank's size, unique prompts, distinct choices, keyed answers, explanations, sources, answer windows, balanced positions and idempotent reseeding. They also verify existing rows and saved selections remain unchanged, and that a Disney & Pixar-only game scores and advances within that topic. Browser acceptance exercises the topic selection and restored session on a phone-sized viewport using actual seeded questions.

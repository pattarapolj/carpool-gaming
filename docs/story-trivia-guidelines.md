# Story trivia guidelines

Movie and TV categories should reward watching or reading the story. A casual fan should regularly recognize an answer, and a car should be able to discuss the clues and make a reasonable guess. Mix familiar facts with moderate challenges; avoid filling a category with specialist production knowledge.

## What to ask

- Characters' goals, relationships, loyalties and choices.
- Recognizable powers, tools, objects, creatures and places.
- What happens in memorable scenes, and why it matters to the story.
- Connections between clues that a viewer or reader can work out.

Give enough context to make one answer correct. Name the film or story situation when continuity matters. Harry Potter follows the seven original books, and Marvel follows MCU films; do not silently combine those with adaptations, comics or supplementary material.

## What to avoid

Director, writer and performer credits; release dates; episode or season identification; runtime, box-office and production statistics; minor passwords or incidental numbers that offer no useful story clue. Important numbers in the story, such as Quidditch rules or the Infinity Stones, can be fair questions when they are familiar and clearly scoped.

Do not restate the same fact as several differently worded questions, or give away the answer in the prompt. Four choices should belong to the same kind of thing and be plausible in the context. Rotate the correct answer's position. The reveal should explain the fact or story connection, rather than merely repeat the answer.

## Reviewing and updating

Review replacements against both retained questions and other replacements for repeated concepts. Check uncertain facts against a relevant plot, character or original-book reference; attribution alone does not establish correctness. Keep source titles and URLs specific, and state verification limits in the bank review.

Released question IDs retain their wording, choices and answers so stored rounds remain readable and can be graded consistently. Replacements get new IDs. The seed transaction marks superseded rows inactive and inserts their replacements together. Future rounds select active questions; archived questions remain available to existing rounds. There are still 300 playable questions per category, while the database also keeps historical rows. Reseeding must preserve both versions without duplicates or overwriting stored edits.
